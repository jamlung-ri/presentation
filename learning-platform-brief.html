Starting Point

We Have Three Advantages Most Platforms Lack

We already operate the model. OHIE Academy has run free-course/paid-cert since 2021 — 750+ learners, 59 certified, at a $22 price point. Expanding it to LOINC, FHIR, and clinical data standards is a scope question, not a proof-of-concept question.

We already publish on a second platform. Regenstrief, GIZ, and Jembi have jointly launched 4 HIS courses on atingi — GIZ's BMZ-funded platform reaching 1.35M+ LMIC development sector learners in 31 languages. The partner channel into that audience already exists.

LOINC is a built-in credentialing anchor. Udemy and Coursera spent years constructing authority through university partnerships. A Regenstrief certificate on LOINC or FHIR carries genuine institutional weight — and could command a meaningfully higher price point ($149–$299) — from day one.

We may not need to build new infrastructure. IU Expand (expand.iu.edu) is Indiana University's Canvas-based continuing-education marketplace — already used across IU schools and departments to sell paid courses and credentials. As part of IU, Regenstrief could explore publishing and selling courses there without standing up a new platform, lowering the risk for an early test of the paid-cert model.

Three Paths

Practical Models for Free Content + Sustainable Revenue

A · Free Content, Paid Certifications
Recommended Starting Point

All courses are free to audit. Learners pay for a verified Regenstrief certificate — e.g., LOINC Practitioner at $149, Clinical Data Standards track at $299, with CME credit where applicable.

Live precedent — OHIE Academy

Regenstrief has run this model since 2021 at academy.ohie.org: 16 free HIE courses, $22 Foundations cert, 59 certified in 2 years. Expanding to LOINC/FHIR is a natural extension.

Works because
  • Aligns with open-access mission
  • RI cert carries real credential value unlike Teachable
  • LOINC + FHIR + HL7 have global demand
  • CME credit adds institutional pull
Watch out for
  • Cert revenue requires volume to sustain
  • Assessment + renewal infrastructure needed
  • Works better with B2B alongside it edX lesson
B · Developer / Creator Publishing Fees
Novel for Health Informatics

RI-adjacent vendors, developers, and academic partners pay to publish under the Regenstrief brand — buying access to our audience and credentialing trust. Learners still get free content.

Works because
  • Learners stay free — mission intact
  • Vendors pay for legitimacy + distribution Udemy model, inverted
  • Scales catalog without adding RI staff
  • Builds ecosystem around RI standards
Watch out for
  • Needs editorial curation policy first
  • Risk of "pay to play" perception
  • RI brand must be protected
C · Free Public + Institutional Licensing
Longer Runway

Content stays free. Health systems and ministries of health pay for organizational access — custom tracks, cohort management, progress dashboards, and LMS integration.

Works because
  • Mirrors how health systems actually buy training Coursera for Business Udemy Business
  • GHI relationships open MoH-level doors
  • High-ticket, low-volume — viable at smaller scale
Watch out for
  • Long institutional sales cycles
  • Requires account management capacity
  • Needs a proven catalog first
What the Landscape Shows

Six Patterns Worth Noting

Free content alone doesn't sustain

edX ran it for 9 years and sold for $800M. OpenWHO works because WHO absorbs costs as infrastructure. Without institutional backing, free needs a revenue layer.

Credentialing is our moat

LOINC, FHIR, HL7 proficiency certs backed by Regenstrief carry weight no commercial platform can replicate — and what makes the paid tier credible.

B2B is the common survival move

Every platform that lasted — Coursera, Udemy Business, Open edX — pivoted to institutional licensing. Individual learner revenue rarely sustains at scale.

The infrastructure already exists

Open edX (free, self-hosted) is what OpenWHO runs on. A Regenstrief instance could be stood up in weeks — the question is staffing and governance, not technology.

atingi — a live LMIC channel

Regenstrief already publishes HIS courses on atingi (GIZ's development sector platform). 1.35M+ learners, 31 languages, zero cost. Expanding health informatics content there requires no new deal.

FutureLearn — governance risk

Academic-founded (Open University UK), sold to private equity in 2022. 18M+ learners but deteriorating partner terms. Lower priority than atingi for LMIC reach or Coursera for credentialing.

Suggested Direction

Start A + B, Build Toward C

Launch with free content + paid Regenstrief certifications (A) to establish the platform and prove demand. Layer in developer publishing fees (B) to grow the catalog without requiring RI staff to author everything. Once the content is real, institutional licensing (C) becomes viable — MoH partners will pay for cohort tools when they can see the curriculum. This is essentially what edX attempted, but our certification authority gives the paid tier value from day one rather than after years of brand-building.

Open Questions

What We Need to Decide

01
What does a Regenstrief certificate mean? RI credential vs. LOINC credential vs. consortium cert — who sets the exam, who renews it?
02
Who are the first learners? Frontline health workers in LMICs (→ free, offline-first), US clinical informaticists (→ CME, institutional), or both?
03
Who can publish? RI staff only? Vetted partners? Any developer for a fee? Curation policy must exist before the doors open.
04
GHI, LOINC, CBMI — or all three? Governance determines who controls the roadmap, owns the brand, and shares in revenue.
05
What's the year-one budget floor? Open edX hosting (~$5–15K/yr) + one course producer + cert program design. How much runway before we need revenue?
