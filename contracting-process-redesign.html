Slide 2

How to Read This

Same spirit as everything else this project has produced.

  • This is a strawman, like every other document in this project. It is meant to be picked apart and corrected, not approved as written.
  • Two threads run through it: what is changing in the process itself, and what is changing in the tools and governance behind it.
  • A lot of what follows comes from live working sessions with the pre-award and post-award team leads, plus five customer interviews. Some of it will be new even to people who have lived this process for years, because it is the first time it has all been written down in one place.
  • Nothing here commits budget or headcount. The goal is to align on direction before we scope initiatives in more detail.

Slide 5

Issues That Cut Across Every Phase

These aren't confined to one part of the lifecycle. Keep them in mind heading into the phase-by-phase review that follows, they account for a lot of what gets marked Not Addressed or Partial there.

No default owner for administrative work. Scheduling, documentation, and following up on outstanding items aren't the science itself, that stays with Science, but nothing assigns this work to Pre-Award, Post-Award, or Contracting either, so it defaults to whichever Science team member is willing to chase it. Sharpest during active award administration (Phase 6), but also shows up at handoff (Phase 5) and in scientific and programmatic activity (Phase 8). Proposed fix: Administrative Task Ownership & Tracking, Slide 26.
No shared status visibility. Nobody outside the person handling a request can see where it stands. Pre-award and post-award have each independently built their own personal tracking spreadsheets to cope, and requesters do the same. Touches nearly every phase, sharpest at submission (Phase 3) and during active award administration (Phase 6). Proposed fix: Shared Status Dashboard, Slide 26.
No defined turnaround-time expectations. Nothing tells a requester or stakeholder what a normal wait looks like, so silence reads as a problem and last-minute questions pile up right before a deadline instead of surfacing early. Shows up at submission (Phase 3), during active award administration (Phase 6), and in invoicing (Phase 7). Proposed fix: Turnaround-Time Expectations, Slide 26.
No single source of truth for documents and data. Contracts, budgets, and obligations live wherever the person handling them happened to save them, a shared drive, an individual desktop, a personal inbox. Shows up in award document execution (Phase 4), at handoff (Phase 5), and in closeout and audit (Phase 10). Proposed fix: Storage & Source of Truth, Slide 26.
Slide 6
Partial

Phase 1: Opportunity Identification & Eligibility

Where a request enters the process, and who owns getting proposals staffed.

No single front door. Investigators and program teams have used a personal inbox, a shared inbox, or the industry-partnership team, depending on who they happened to ask, with a different experience down each path.
Workload is balanced through a personal tool, not a documented standard. A staff-built scoring system rates each proposal's complexity (sub-only vs. prime-plus-sub vs. multiple subs) and feeds a workload total per person. It works, but it would not transfer to a new hire.
Coordination with the industry-partnership team is a confirmed gap, acknowledged on both sides, not yet resolved.
Whether a PI has multiple submissions out at once isn't tracked anywhere in pre-award. It's treated as a downstream, post-award concern instead.
Slide 7
Not Addressed

Phase 2: Proposal Development & Budget

Where the narrative and budget actually get built.

No low-stakes way to rough out numbers before the official budget process starts. Program staff have been told not to informally play with percentages at all, even just to sanity-check a figure.
Budget calculation errors happen, and there's no documented quality check. One historical near-miss: unconsulted market-rate salary increases nearly bankrupted grants years ago. The fix, budgeting at a mid-level rate, is informal practice, not a documented step.
A separate budget-related form duplicates work already captured in the main request, so the same numbers get provided twice, to two different teams.
Fair-market-value budgeting for industry-funded work follows a completely different process from standard grant budgeting, and it isn't represented in the documented process at all.
Slide 8
Not Addressed

Phase 3: Submission & Sponsor Review

Once a proposal is out the door, until an award decision comes back.

A sponsor request for updated information can resurface a proposal presumed dead over a year after submission, with no shared record of why it was written the way it was. Two live examples exist right now.
Status monitoring is opportunistic, not active. Sponsor communication about a proposal usually reaches the investigator first, so pre-award often learns of changes only by asking.

This phase is also where two cross-cutting issues hit hardest: no shared status visibility, and no defined turnaround-time expectations, see Slide 5.

Slide 9
Partial

Phase 4: Award Document Execution

Reviewing and executing the actual award document.

A single reviewer handles essentially all contract review. That is a real, current bottleneck, not a hypothetical one, and coverage has already been tested by one extended absence.
No unified way exists to check whether an agreement with a given partner already exists. Staff have assumed one was already in place with a partner organization, checked, and found there wasn't one.
Debarment and suspension screening, and the independent-contractor check, both currently happen after this step, once the award has already landed. That timing is being questioned: shouldn't RI confirm a named vendor is workable before including them in a proposal, rather than after?
Slide 10
Partial

Phase 5: Award Setup & Handoff

Getting a newly awarded project properly set up and handed to the team that runs it day to day.

No formal handoff meeting exists in practice. A standing biweekly meeting between the two teams covers it informally instead.
Budgets don't reliably travel with the award. Sometimes the file isn't in the expected shared-drive location; sometimes it's sitting on an individual's desktop.
Vendor documentation can be missing at handoff. Projects routed through an internal service unit have reached the next team with only a verbal budget number, no draft agreement, quote, or the vendor's correct legal name.

Planned

Phase 6: Active Award Administration

The ongoing, monthly work of running an award once it's active. Most of this phase's pain is systemic rather than unique to it, see Slide 5.

Ad hoc, cross-team requests that fall outside the well-defined recurring tasks (an unusual data pull, for example) land on whoever happens to be available, not a defined owner.

Not Addressed

Phase 7: Invoicing & Financial Compliance

Generating and reconciling invoices against each award's terms.

The monthly invoicing update is often delayed, especially when a contract isn't fully set up yet, and it isn't always clear when an update has happened.
Indirect-rate calculation errors come up often enough that staff describe being afraid to touch the spreadsheet, worried they'll break something they don't fully understand.
No explicit quality check exists anywhere in the invoicing or budget-scenario chain to catch these errors before they reach a sponsor.

Planned

Phase 8: Scientific & Programmatic Activity

The research and reporting work that runs alongside every other phase, not after them.

Investigators describe needing to act as scientist, project manager, and contracting expert all at once, more roles than one person should have to carry.
This is sharpest in industry-partner work, which is newer territory for most teams and far less supported today than standard federal-grant work.
Slide 14
Not Addressed

Phase 9: Continuation, Amendments & Supplemental Funding

Extending, amending, or adding funding to an award already in progress.

A recent reassignment of simple continuing-award work to a different team was decided above both teams, not agreed between them, and pre-award has pushed back on it more than once.
Whether the underlying responsibility matrix is a final decision or still a working draft has never been resolved, despite being raised three separate times.
Proposed contract changes are often exchanged by email outside the tracking system, with no shared way to see which version is current.
Slide 15
Not Addressed

Phase 10: Closeout & Audit

Wrapping up a completed award, and keeping track of what it still obligates RI to do.

Renewal dates and other obligations are tracked by hand: someone opens the signed PDF, finds the date, and manually calculates a deadline. It's easy to misread a date or simply forget.
Nothing links related agreements together for reporting. Executing an award doesn't automatically flag a related vendor agreement or lease named in the same proposal.
Portfolio-level reporting, such as what's up for renewal next quarter, is only as reliable as whatever's been typed into a spreadsheet by hand.

The Shift We're Making

Zooming into a single contracting request as it actually moves, today versus tomorrow, once a need for one exists. This cuts across several phases from the map above, most often Phases 1, 4, 6, and 9.

Triage & Routing
Drafting & Internal Review

Today
Three uncoordinated inboxes.
Triaged by feel, no documented tiering.
Pre/post-award responsibilities still being worked out.
Proposed changes exchanged by email, no version tracking.
RI doesn't own Adobe Sign; no return notification.

What Changes
  • Retire the personal inbox.
  • Split into two shared inboxes, pre-award and post-award, each with a backup owner.
  • Adopt a routing rule based on funding source and flow-down status.
  • Add a weekly cross-team meeting to set top priorities.
  • Publish the responsibility document.
  • Move to parallel approvals, for example Finance and Legal together, instead of one after another.
  • Move proposed contract changes into Issuetrak instead of email.
  • Track versions so nothing gets lost between them.
  • Broaden signer access.
  • Fix the notification gap when a signed copy returns.
  • Centralize storage and build dashboards.
  • Link contracts so obligations track automatically.
Future
Two shared inboxes, no personal inbox.
Rule-based routing, weekly priority-setting.
Documented responsibilities, parallel approvals.
Tracked workflow, no lost versions.
Broader access, reliable notification.
Centralized, automatic tracking.
Slide 25

The Tooling Decision Is Already Made, Mostly

Leadership chose Issuetrak in mid-August. The known gaps were deferred, not resolved, and one objection behind that choice turned out to be wrong.

Issuetrak was chosen over two AI-native contract lifecycle systems (Ironclad and LinkSquares) in mid-August 2026. Rationale: the team already knows it. Get the process itself defined and working there first, then see what gaps remain, rather than pick a system before the process is settled.
Real, acknowledged gap, deferred rather than fixed: Issuetrak has no native contract data model, no e-signature, and no AI review. It's a ticket log with a reporting widget attached, not a contract lifecycle management system.
Ironclad and LinkSquares both scored 18 to 20 of 20 core contract-lifecycle capabilities in the same comparison, including native status dashboards and obligation and renewal tracking, the exact gap this whole project exists to close.
The access concern that originally weighed against them was a misunderstanding. The real requirement is IU single sign-on login, not network-level VPN-gating. Both vendors support standard single sign-on, the same protocol IU's identity system already runs on. That objection no longer holds as stated.
Two more tech-stack facts worth knowing: RI doesn't own its own Adobe Sign account, IU does. And Financial Edge is staying exactly as-is on purpose, to avoid stacking another system change on top of the incoming HR system and a recent HR-platform rollout.

Proposed Initiatives (Strawman)

Grouped by what they change: process, technology, or governance, the same three threads from Slide 2. Sizes are a starting guess, not a commitment.

Process
Consolidate IntakeS
Formalize the two shared inboxes and assign a backup person to each. Retire the personal inbox for good.
Screening Timing FixS
Move debarment/suspension screening and the independent-contractor check to pre-award, before submission. Proposed by the post-award team, not yet tested against pre-award's own view.
Pre / Post-Award Handoff DocsS
Finalize and publish the responsibility document already in draft. Consider a monthly audit to catch budgets that go missing in the handoff.
Write a Post-Award SOPM
Give post-award the same real, documented backbone pre-award already has. The first attempt failed because nobody who owns the work was consulted.
Investigator EducationM
Train investigators, especially in GHI, on contracting basics. Pair training with real accountability.
Budget & Invoicing QA StepS
Add an explicit quality check to catch indirect-rate and budget calculation errors before they reach post-award or a sponsor. Doesn't fix the separate problem of budget work being split across disconnected forms, see the Decisions slide.
Administrative Task Ownership & TrackingM
Not the science itself, that stays with Science. This is the scheduling, documentation, and follow-up work that comes up throughout a project and doesn't have a default owner today, so it falls to whichever Science team member is willing to chase it. Give it a default home across Pre-Award, Post-Award, and Contracting, and track it to completion. Already named as a gap in the future-state design work: assigning tasks and deadlines, not just status, is closer to what a real project-management tool does than what a ticket system is built for.
Technology
Shared Status DashboardM
Give requesters and program staff a live view of where a request stands. The single most-requested fix across the interviews, and already named as a future-state goal, but never scoped as its own initiative until now. Needs a paired owner who keeps it current, or it becomes another stale artifact like today's invoicing workbook.
NDA & Agreement Lookup ListS
A scoped idea already on the table: a published list of organization, document type, and expiration date. Answers "do we already have one" without opening document access org-wide.
Adobe Sign FixesS
Broaden who can run signature requests and close the notification gap when a signed copy returns.
Storage & Source of TruthM
Centralize contract storage, link Financial Edge master agreements to their statements of work by funder, and define who can see what.
Negotiation WorkflowM
Define how proposed contract changes move in and out of Issuetrak, with version tracking, instead of living only in email.
Obligation & Compliance TrackingL
Replace manual date math with structured tracking of renewals, auto-renewal windows, and data-privacy terms.
Governance
Risk-Based Routing & PrioritizationM
Adopt the funding-source routing rule and stand up a recurring weekly meeting to set top priorities across the org.
Reduce the Single-Reviewer BottleneckM
One person reviewing essentially all contracts is a live risk right now, not a hypothetical one. Decide whether a second trained reviewer is temporary coverage or a structural need.
Turnaround-Time ExpectationsS
Post-award has no defined response-time commitments at all, unlike pre-award's incomplete SLA table. Publish expected turnaround by request type, so silence doesn't read as a problem and questions surface early instead of right before a deadline.
Slide 27

Decisions This Group Actually Needs to Make

  • Is the underlying responsibility matrix a final decision or still a working draft? This has come up three separate times independently, in a working session with the post-award team, in pre-award's internal pushback, and in the post-award team lead raising it directly with leadership, without ever being settled.
  • Should debarment/suspension screening and the independent-contractor check move to pre-award, before submission? The post-award team believes so. Not yet tested against pre-award's own view.
  • Do we revisit the AI-native contract lifecycle options now that the access objection turns out to be a misunderstanding, or stay committed to building the process out in Issuetrak first?
  • Who resolves Adobe Sign account ownership with IU, and on what timeline?
  • Do Operational contracts (vendor agreements, software licensing, not tied to an award) sit with the current contracting team, or somewhere else? Today staff are only assigned to federal work.
  • What's the real distinction between private-funder and industry contracts? It isn't clear to the group itself yet.
  • Is a monthly workflow audit worth requiring, to catch pre-award to post-award handoff gaps before they become a problem?
  • When an administrative task doesn't clearly belong to Pre-Award, Post-Award, or Contracting, who holds default ownership, so it stops defaulting to whichever Science team member is willing to chase it?
  • Who owns budget ownership across the intake form, the separate RDS form, and the fair-market-value process for industry work? Right now they're disconnected enough that requesters sometimes act as the go-between reconciling numbers across teams themselves.
