Recap

The problem OCL Mapper set out to solve

  • Terminology mapping is a major bottleneck for interoperability worldwide
  • LOINC's high specificity makes mapping especially hard — missing metadata limits automation and pushes people toward generic codes, creating downstream risk
  • RELMA is still the gold standard, but shows signs of age
  • Mapping is usually done by non-experts; many Ministries of Health don't have a terminologist on staff
  • No solution existed that was open source, collaborative, terminology-agnostic, and AI-enabled
Source: condensed from APHL deck slide 8.

What's new · go deeper here

ICD-11 results and bridge terminology

60%exact-match accuracy from the CIEL bridge alone — nearly matching the full 4-algorithm system (59%)
+18point lift in exact-match accuracy from adding the CIEL bridge to a baseline API
14correct codes found only by the CIEL bridge — no other algorithm surfaced them
  • The curated bridge terminology (CIEL) is the single most valuable, least-replaceable piece of the system — it carries most of the exact-match accuracy on its own. Generic search APIs and an AI agent add breadth, not precision
  • Direct relevance to this room: that's the same bridge mechanism (CIEL) behind LOINC mapping — this is the real story for LOINC
  • When the right answer was in the candidate pool, the AI Assistant picked it ~99% of the time — the limit is retrieval, not judgment
  • The AI also knows when to say "I don't know": on thin candidate pools it abstained 22 times, and was right to abstain 86% of the time
Source: GDHF 2026 Bangkok abstract draft (accepted) — 100 high-frequency clinical/lab terms, expert-mapped to ICD-11, evaluated against OCL Mapper. Preliminary / pre-publication, single source (IMO Health) — say so if asked about sample size. This is the section to spend a couple of extra minutes on if time allows.
