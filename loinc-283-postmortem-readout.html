Finding 1

External commitments are not tracked

Two real cases (submissions 8097 and 8144) sat untracked for months because commitments were made by email or verbally, with no register anywhere answering “what did we promise, to whom, by when.”

Root cause and fix

Decided
The team did not build new tooling. The existing signals were judged sufficient — the actual gap was that priority levels have no shared, written meaning, and sometimes went unassigned entirely.
  • Priority Level + Target Release field in LMS are the two signals we use — no new tag or field needed on top of them. A per-release commitment dashboard/board (new this cycle, e.g. tagging a submission to "2.83-1") already worked well and stays in use.
  • Write down shared definitions for each priority level, so “critical” / “high” / “medium” mean the same thing to everyone. Owner: Eza · Due: Sep 3, 2026
  • Build out the priority/commitment list itself (the per-release dashboard). Owner: April · Due: Sep 3, 2026
  • Standing practice, effective immediately: whoever makes or receives a commitment — by any channel, email, Help Scout, or verbal — logs it into LMS notes with channel and date. LMS is the source of truth, not email or meeting recall.
Confirm: draft priority-level definitions shared before Sep 3, agreed at the all-team meeting that day.
Finding 2 · not in the original deck

Submitter communication lapse — Roche / submission 7720

An HPV submission from Roche went ~10 weeks and 5 follow-ups with zero internal signal anything was wrong, until it escalated outside LOINC to a Roche executive contacting Regenstrief leadership directly.

Immediate action

Decided
  • Create the terms for submission 7720 via the pre-release process, so Roche has something in hand before the scheduled call. Owner: Geoffrey (assigned by Eza) · Due: Tue Sep 1, 2026

Root causes identified

Agreed
  • No support thread was actually linked to the submission — LMS held the work, Help Scout held the conversation (across 4 separate threads), nothing joined them.
  • Closing a Help Scout ticket was treated as an administrative action, not confirmation a real response went out.
  • A QA question needing submitter input was recorded internally but never sent to the submitter.
  • “On hold” status is visible to the submitter but undefined — no review date, unclear if it's terminal.
  • Same root cause as Finding 1: priority/escalation level was never assigned, so it fell under the radar.

Root fix: retire Help Scout for submissions

In progress
Decided direction, not yet built. Jira was explicitly considered and rejected (cost, cloud-only, vendor practices, painful API, too complex for the need).
  • Submissions get decoupled from Help Scout entirely. A custom front end, with LMS as the back end, threaded by submission ID. Requires organizational (not individual) accounts, so departing staff don't leave submitters emailing dead addresses.
  • Submitters go through the portal directly, not email — an email reply to a notification bounces back with a redirect to the portal.
  • Content-edit requests that arrive by email outside the normal process (e.g. a stakeholder repeatedly emailing 20–30 edits directly) get converted into tracked submissions/tasks rather than handled as one-off email threads.
  • Next concrete step is a core requirements list, not a full build — scope (lightweight comms thread vs. fuller case management) isn't agreed yet. Owner: Joe · Due: not yet set
Confirm: is Joe's requirements list still the right next step, and does it need a target date?
