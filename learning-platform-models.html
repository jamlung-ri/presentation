Business models, financing, and governance — a landscape scan to inform a Regenstrief-centric health informatics learning platform.

GHI · LOINC / HDS · CBMI · Regenstrief · Joe Amlung
Context

What would it take to build a Regenstrief-centric learning platform — and who has done it before?

This scan covers how platforms like Udemy, Coursera, edX, OpenWHO, FutureLearn, atingi (GIZ), Teachable, and Open edX finance themselves, share revenue with creators, govern content, and scale infrastructure — with direct implications for GHI, LOINC/HDS, and CBMI.

The Landscape

Seven Platform Archetypes

01
Commercial Marketplace
Udemy — crowdsourced instructors, per-course sales, platform keeps 63% of organic revenue
02
Enterprise Subscription (B2B)
Coursera for Business, Udemy Business — bulk access sold to companies; dominant revenue driver; combined platform targets ~$1.5B
03
Academic / Nonprofit MOOC
Original edX (MIT + Harvard) — free audits, certificate upsell, institutionally governed; never reached financial sustainability alone
04
Mission-Funded Public Good
OpenWHO — grant/budget funded, free, offline-capable, 75 languages, 9.2M enrollments over 7 years
05
Hosted SaaS Creator Tool
Teachable, Thinkific — org pays flat monthly SaaS fee ($39–$400), keeps 100% of course revenue, full IP control
06
Development Sector Grant Platform
atingi (GIZ) — BMZ-funded, free to all, 1.35M+ learners in 31 languages, open to partner content publishing; Regenstrief already publishes here
07
PE-Acquired MOOC
FutureLearn — academic-founded (Open University UK), sold to private equity 2022; 18M+ learners; freemium + subscription; governance risk for academic partners

Research Question 3 · The Cautionary Tale

The edX Story

2012
MIT + Harvard launch edX
Nonprofit, mission-driven, free MOOCs for global learners
2018
Sustainability crisis
Certificate upsell alone couldn't sustain platform scale; began experimenting with paid tiers
2021
Sold to 2U Inc. for ~$800M
Proceeds → new nonprofit (Center for Reimagining Learning) now stewarding Open edX platform
Now
Open edX survives as open source
Community-governed, Apache-style; institutions self-host or use commercial partners (e.g., edunext)
Lesson

Nonprofit + MOOC scale is very hard without an endowment or a B2B pivot. The open-source platform survived — the nonprofit MOOC brand did not.

What survived

Open edX is now consortium-governed with fragmented but resilient community support. This is a viable model for Regenstrief — but requires dev/ops investment to self-host.

The Health Analog

OpenWHO — Closest Model for Regenstrief

→
Launched 2017 by WHO
Built on Open edX; hosted by Hasso Plattner Institute on private OpenStack cloud
→
Free, offline-capable, 75 languages
Self-paced; designed for low-bandwidth frontline responders in any country or territory
→
9.2M enrollments, 315+ courses in 7 years
Just-in-time emergency response learning for COVID, Ebola, mpox, and more
→
Funded by WHO budget + HPI partnership
Zero tuition, zero subscription — sustained as public health infrastructure, not a product
For Regenstrief

If framed as global health informatics infrastructure, this model is the most mission-aligned — but requires sustained institutional budget commitment or large grant backing (Gates Foundation, Wellcome, NIH).

We Already Run This Model

OHIE Academy — Regenstrief's Own Precedent

→
Launched January 2021
Regenstrief Institute manages the Academy as secretariat of OpenHIE; grew out of in-person training at the 2019 Addis Ababa community meeting
→
16 free, self-paced courses
Covering HIE architecture, identity management, OpenHIE community concepts — free to all registered learners
→
Paid certification: $22
Foundations of Health Information Exchange — 27 MCQs, max 2 attempts, downloadable certificate
→
750+ users · 59 certifications (2-year mark)
Small but real — proof the free-course/paid-cert model works in Regenstrief's hands at a global health informatics audience
The key insight

We don't need to look to OpenWHO or edX as aspirational models — we already operate one. The question is whether to expand the OHIE Academy model to cover LOINC, FHIR, clinical data standards, and CBMI content, and how to fund that expansion.

What's different at scale

Credentialing authority grows with scope. A LOINC Practitioner cert or FHIR proficiency badge from Regenstrief would carry substantially more weight than the current HIE Foundations cert — and could command a higher price point.

The Missing Platforms

atingi & FutureLearn

atingi (GIZ) — already our partner

GIZ, Jembi, and Regenstrief have already launched 4 HIS courses on atingi — GIZ's BMZ-funded platform reaching 1.35M+ LMIC development sector learners in 31 languages. Free to all, open to partner organizations, zero cost to publish.

For Regenstrief

atingi is the fastest path to LMIC scale at zero cost. The partnership infrastructure is live. Expanding health informatics content here requires no new platform deal — just more courses.

FutureLearn — governance risk

Founded by The Open University (UK) in 2012; sold to Global University Systems (private equity) in 2022. 18M+ learners, 260+ partner institutions. Freemium + subscription model. Health content present but not a dominant focus.

For Regenstrief

The learner base is significant — but PE ownership since 2022 means partner terms are less stable than academic-governed alternatives. Lower-priority option versus atingi for LMIC reach or Coursera for credentialing weight.

Research Question 5

What Scaling Actually Requires

01
Content production
SME time, instructional design, QA — Udemy crowdsources 73,000+ instructors; OpenWHO uses WHO staff
02
Translation & localization
OpenWHO supports 75 languages with volunteer translation — a major, ongoing, underestimated cost
03
Infrastructure
CDN-distributed or private cloud; offline access for low-bandwidth; LMS integrations for enterprise deployments
04
Credentialing
What does a certificate mean? Who recognizes it? Coursera has university backing; WHO has global public health authority
For LOINC / HDS specifically

Credentialing is the highest-value differentiator. A LOINC or FHIR proficiency certificate with Regenstrief backing carries institutional weight that a Teachable course cannot replicate — and is a genuine moat.

Next Steps

Questions to Bring to the Room

01
Product or public infrastructure?
Is the goal revenue generation, or a global health informatics public good funded by grants/institutional budget?
02
Who are the primary learners?
Frontline health workers (→ OpenWHO model), institutions (→ B2B), or independent researchers (→ Coursera partnership)?
03
IP and credentialing ownership?
Who owns the curriculum? What does a certificate mean, and who backs it — Regenstrief alone, or a consortium?
04
Multi-stakeholder governance?
Regenstrief-only, or a consortium across GHI, LOINC/HDS, and CBMI — each contributing content and co-governing decisions?
05
Sustainable funding floor?
What's the minimum viable revenue or grant coverage to sustain content QA, infrastructure, and translation over 5+ years?
