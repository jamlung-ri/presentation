Extended Family & Friends Guide
A one-page resource families can share so their support network knows how to actually help
Who uses it
Mothers & partners share with grandparents, siblings, close friends
When
At or shortly after diagnosis — coordinator hands to family
Format
Print card · PDF · shareable link
Effort
Low
Adapts existing palliative-care template
Origin
Requested by name by Megan Bess (4/8 session)
Concept sketch — what this might look like
Supporting a Family with a CHD Diagnosis  —  A Guide for Family & Friends Riley Children's Heart Center  ·  rileychildrens.org/heart
✓ Things that genuinely help
✓
"We're here — what do you need right now?"
Offers presence without adding to their to-do list
✓
"Can I bring dinner on Tuesday?"
Specific, practical — easy to say yes to
✓
"I don't know what to say — but I'm not going anywhere."
Honest presence is more helpful than forced optimism
✓
"I'll handle [specific thing] so you don't have to think about it."
Takes a task without asking them to manage you
✓
"Do you want to talk about it, or talk about anything else?"
Gives them control over whether they re-explain the diagnosis — again
✕ Well-meaning things that often hurt
✕
"Everything happens for a reason."
Even if you believe this, it's not what someone in crisis needs to hear
✕
"At least you caught it early" / "At least it's fixable."
Minimizes real fear — their child has a serious heart defect
✕
"Have you tried...?" / "What about...?"
They have a team. Unsolicited advice adds to the noise
✕
"What do the doctors think caused this?"
Often unanswerable and can feel like blame
✕
Asking for updates constantly — "any news?"
Every update request is a task for them. Let them share when ready
📋
For care coordinators: This resource can be printed and given at the first appointment, emailed as a PDF, or texted as a link — however is most natural for your workflow. The mother decides who to share it with.
Why this matters in the dataExtended-family contact appeared in 20+ coded instances — frequently as a burden, not a support. Mothers were "exhausted from repeatedly re-explaining the diagnosis to well-meaning relatives."
Low-effort path to buildMegan noted Riley already has a similar guide for palliative patients. This would adapt that resource for cardiology — not start from scratch.
Could stand alone or feed a bigger toolWorks as a standalone handout now, and as a shareable resource inside the mother app concept (Concept 2) later.
What's ready to discuss
Does existing palliative template need major changes? Who drafts and clinically reviews language? Print vs. digital vs. both?
Open questions
Riley branding / approval process? How does coordinator know when to hand it out?

🔀 Phased, readiness-based orientation
Mirrors what coordinators do in person — doesn't dump all information at once. Adapts to where the family is in the journey.
📚 Curated, vetted resource directory
The "trusted starting points" coordinators said they need but don't have bandwidth to build. Candidates already named: pted.org, Mended Little Hearts, Sisters by Heart, CHD Care Compass via NAFNET.
👥 Guided peer-connection routing
Some Facebook groups help, some frighten. This routes mothers toward vetted peer support — the single strongest resilience pattern in the study (CTR-4: Maintaining Communication Networks, 34 coded instances).
🏥 Practical prep content
Care conferences, NICU stays, what to pack, what the first Riley appointment actually looks like (it's an all-day multi-provider event — most families didn't know).
88% of mothers in the study needed psychosocial support. Most did not receive it. There are no formal guidelines for psychosocial support after prenatal CHD diagnosis. This tool fills that gap in the most accessible window.
What's ready to discuss

Open questions
Who maintains it after the grant period? Clinical/liability boundaries for a chatbot layer? Riley-specific vs. regional scope?

When

Effort
Medium

Origin
Dr. Markham's proposal, June 8 meeting
Concept sketch — what might sit in an OB waiting room or exam room
A resource for OB offices  ·  When a possible heart finding is detected on anatomy scan Counter card / display piece — draft concept
The situation
If your OB finds a possible heart finding, you have more options than you may realize.
Families are often referred to the nearest large hospital — or wherever the OB's system is affiliated — without knowing that where you go for CHD care can significantly affect outcomes.

Surgical volume, specialized team experience, and proximity to home all matter. You are allowed to ask about them. You are allowed to choose.
Questions worth asking your OB
  • Is the center you're referring me to a surgical center for congenital heart disease — or a diagnostic center?
  • How many CHD cases does that program handle per year? (Volume is linked to outcomes.)
  • Is there a closer center with comparable experience I should know about?
  • Can I speak with a care coordinator there before I decide?
  • What does the care team look like — is there dedicated CHD coordination, MFM, and cardiology together?

ℹ️
For OBs and referring providers: Research shows families trust provider authority and rarely question referral decisions. This resource isn't about redirecting — it's about giving families language to engage in the conversation. A more informed family asks better questions regardless of where they go.
Dr. Markham's core problemLocal OBs refer based on institutional affiliations, not on program outcomes data. Families "aren't sophisticated enough to ask why they can't go to Riley" — and deeply trust whoever gives them the first diagnosis.
Where it fits in the pathwayThis intervenes at Stage 3 (Diagnosis & Provider Response) — the earliest fork in the two maternal pathways finding. A bad first referral can put a family on the isolated pathway before Riley ever gets to engage them.
Political sensitivity to discussComparative program information is delicate — this can't read as disparaging Parkview, Cincinnati, or other systems Riley has ongoing relationships with. Framing and tone will need careful input from Riley's communications team.
What's ready to discuss
Does something like this already exist informally? Where in the OB workflow would it actually land? Paper card vs. QR-to-digital vs. both?
Open questions
Riley-branded vs. neutral statewide vs. CDC-affiliated? How to distribute into OB offices without active health system partnership? Who reviews for political/clinical accuracy?
